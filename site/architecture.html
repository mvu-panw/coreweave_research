Layered view, with PANW enforcement points

Purple pills are proposed PANW enforcement points. The zones, fabric, DPU/Nimbus and Falcon details are Confirmed from CoreWeave docs. The vendor at the edge and the OT and field details are Inferred, so verify them in discovery.

Three security zones (CoreWeave's own model)

  • Zone 0 is DPU management and the Kubernetes control plane. Zone 1 is the application data plane. Zone 2 is external ingress and egress. Confirmed security architecture
  • "The DPU filters north-south traffic using Layer 4 and optional Layer 7 policies. The CNI (such as Cilium with eBPF) manages east-west traffic, enforced at the DPU level."
  • DPUs export logs and metrics. Telemetry runs through VictoriaMetrics and Loki, with customer-deployed Falco or Tetragon for runtime enforcement.
  • Shared responsibility: CoreWeave owns the physical, network, VPC and encapsulation layers. Customers own Kubernetes network policies and guest firewall configuration. model
  • Implication: CoreWeave already built its tenant firewall in software on the DPU. PANW's CoreWeave-owned opportunity is Zone 2, Zone 0, OT and corporate. Zone 1 is a tenant sale. Inferred

Nimbus: CoreWeave's DPU control plane

  • This is proprietary control and data-plane software "integrated into the NVIDIA BlueField DPU that's affixed to each of our Nodes". It has an Operator on the DPU and a Controller in the CKS management cluster. Confirmed docs
  • It offloads "firewalling and encryption directly to the DPU", manages VPC membership (one node can sit in several customer VPCs), and verifies DPU firmware and configuration.
  • Routing is a Layer 3 EVPN-VXLAN overlay that moves intelligence off the TOR. "TOR switches are repurposed as mere packet-forwarding devices, using unnumbered BGP."
  • Why it matters: Cisco's Hybrid Mesh Firewall and Prisma AIRS on BlueField both want a slot on this DPU. CoreWeave controls it, so any DPU security story is a joint-engineering conversation, not a product drop-in. Inferred

The Global AI Network (backbone)

  • CoreWeave runs its own backbone connecting 51 active data centers in North America and Europe (August 11, 2026). It is "Petabit-scale", built on "high speed edge routers", and "every CoreWeave PoP is deployed with multiple 400 Gbps connections over a unified optical IP network". Confirmed backbone
  • MACsec "from day 1 everywhere", end-to-end across the backbone and not only on Direct Connect links. Every PoP connects to at least two others over four physically diverse paths, and every region is dual-homed to two PoPs.
  • New PoPs from Q4 2026: UAE, India (240 MW in Navi Mumbai), Indonesia (three DCs), Singapore, Australia, South Korea and Japan, over terrestrial and subsea fiber. There is also a new 22.5 MW site in Copenhagen.
  • Implication: the PoPs are where internet transit and peering meet a backbone CoreWeave owns. That's where a terabit-class NGFW belongs, and each new APAC or ME PoP is a fresh design. Which vendor makes the edge routers isn't public, so ask. Inferred

Facility OT is software-defined now

  • Valvey is a patent-pending programmable per-rack liquid-cooling valve assembly that controls flow, temperature, pressure and leak detection. Requests to "optimize cooling, isolate a rack for maintenance, or trigger an emergency shutdown" run through it automatically. Confirmed Valvey/Racky
  • Racky aggregates rack power, cooling and environmental sensors, and controls valves and power functions. Both are part of Mission Control and now extend to the Spectrum-6 switch racks.
  • DC systems engineering builds pipelines between IT and OT (DCIM, BMS/EPMS, SCADA). A dedicated OT network security engineer is being hired to set the segmentation standard. job
  • Implication: software that can shut down a rack's cooling is reachable from IT, so it needs OT-grade segmentation and monitoring. This is the strongest greenfield argument on the account. Inferred

Edge and interconnect: what sizes the firewall

  • Dedicated Direct Connect: 10G, 100G or 400G cross-connects with MACsec, LACP bonding into LAGs, and BGP with MD5 or TCP-AO. Virtual DX is 10G through Equinix Fabric or Megaport, with no MACsec. Confirmed docs
  • CoreWeave Interconnect (Q2 2026): private fiber to Google Cloud, with other hyperscalers to follow. Cross-cloud training data and checkpoints become a north-south flow. Q2
  • Node north-south NIC: dual-port 200GbE on B300, GB300 and Vera Rubin, so a full NVL72 rack (18 nodes) can push up to 7.2 Tb/s out of the rack. In practice the bottleneck is the AZ uplink, not the node.
  • Public IPs: each General Access AZ has a /22 or larger public block plus /24 NAT egress pools. Every customer LoadBalancer is internet-facing by default. US-EAST

Identity, keys and access

  • CoreWeave IAM: RBAC/ABAC across the Console, API, Terraform, Grafana and Object Storage. SAML for the Console, OIDC for CKS, and SCIM user provisioning from Okta or Entra. docs
  • SUNK User Provisioning turns a federated user into a POSIX user with SSH keys on Slurm login nodes in under a minute. blog
  • Support Access Management gates CoreWeave staff access to dedicated, single-tenant nodes. Teleport is the likely engine behind CoreWeave's own operator access. Inferred
  • Remote Key Encryption: customer keys stay in HashiCorp Vault Enterprise or a KMIP KMS/HSM, so "CoreWeave never sees plaintext".
