Few public policy debates have grown so loud so quickly as the one over data centers. 
The scrutiny is understandable and necessary. 

But somewhere in the noise clear facts can get lost: not all data center developers engage and build with communities in the same way.
At CoreWeave, we operate with a simple principle: communities that make our operations possible should be able to share in the benefits without shouldering our costs.
Our belief is that every data center is part of a community, whether it sits in the plains, in the sun-soaked Southwest, or in our own home state of New Jersey. Every facility depends on local land, energy, water, roads, public institutions, and, ultimately, public trust.This is why  meaningful community engagement is not an afterthought at CoreWeave. It runs from the earliest conversations through ongoing operations.
So it’s promising to see others are also adopting these principles. This week, more data center developers, utilities, and state leaders signed the U.S. Ratepayer Protection Pledge. The pledge asks signatories to fully fund their own power supply and grid upgrades so electricity costs don’t get passed along to everyday ratepayers.
It was easy for CoreWeave to sign on, because it’s simply a reaffirmation of how we engage and build today.  
What’s noteworthy in the newest crop of signatories is the signal a growing coalition  sends: that building the infrastructure for American AI leadership requires broad partnership across industry, government, utilities, and the communities where that infrastructure takes root.

## Genuine engagement
Data centers can bring significant investment, good-paying jobs, new tax revenue and improvements to critical infrastructure. Once built, the benefits cascade across local economies and within research communities, powering breakthroughs and innovations in business, science, medicine, and beyond.
Neither the benefits nor the risks are inevitable. They're shaped by how communities and policymakers engage, how utilities plan for power, and how builders design, communicate, and pay for what they build.

## Our commitment
The right question is not whether data centers are inherently good or bad. It is whether a project has been designed with local partnership and with long-term benefits considered from the start.
At CoreWeave, that is the standard we believe our industry should meet. Making sure communities don't bear the energy costs of AI infrastructure is one essential piece of that social contract. But it’s only one.
Responsible development also means conserving local resources, designing with neighbors, creating real economic opportunity, and staying accountable long after construction ends. At CoreWeave, that commitment begins before the first shovel and outlasts the construction crews, making the community stronger for the long term.
